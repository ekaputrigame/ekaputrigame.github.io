Average FPS is a lie: two weeks of frame-time logs

28 October 2026 · Eka Putri

Almost every phone review gives you one number, the average frame rate, and then moves on. I wanted to understand why a handset that holds a respectable 58 fps still feels sticky under the thumb, so for two weeks I logged frame times instead of frame rates.

Setup: one mid-range Android phone, an overlay sampling every frame, and twenty-minute sessions in three games — a 60 fps shooter, an open-world RPG locked at 30, and a racing title. Per session I logged the one-percent low, the frames delivered later than 32 ms, and any stall over 200 ms. Each test ran once in a 25 °C room, once after forty minutes of play.

Three things surprised me. First, the average barely moved while the late-frame count tripled, which is exactly why the averages are useless. Second, the worst stutters were not in the busiest fights: they clustered in the first ninety seconds of a match, while shaders compiled, and right after a map load, when storage woke up. Third, capping the game at 45 fps looked smoother than leaving it unlocked at 60 and letting it dip.

So: if a phone feels bad but benchmarks look fine, watch the frame-time graph, not the counter. The raw logs and the longer write-ups live at my game notes.
